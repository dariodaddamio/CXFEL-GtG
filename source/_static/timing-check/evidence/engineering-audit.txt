## What this means for the LED/camera test

**We have not measured camera frames per LED flash. No new camera images or video were acquired during Steps 1–3.** The completed result only establishes that two EVRs reported the same timestamps for a shared electronic event stream. It does not establish exposure/flash overlap or camera-event association.

A subsequent read-only camera audit found all four cameras idle and configured for camera-clock timestamps. Camera 20 retained frame 1707 from October 9; cameras 30/40 retained frames from September 25. Cached camera image time values are not on the EVR POSIX time base. The cached images were retrieved and labeled as historical under camera-audit/; none is a frame from the timing test.

The current first event-101 timestamp is 53.203928 ms after PPS. EVR 10 output 1 is configured for event 125 with a 50 microsecond pulse; EVR 20 output 1 is configured for event 101. Camera 20 is set to Line1-triggered FrameStart with a 1 ms exposure. IF the former drives the LED and the latter triggers camera 20, their windows would not overlap. That physical wiring, any DG645 delays and LED location in the image remain unverified.

The next useful experiment is a bounded camera-20 capture with its existing trigger settings, saving every new frame and its metadata alongside the existing event logs. It will establish whether images arrive and whether a flash is visible. No camera PV writes have been made; acquisition still requires exact-command approval. A flash-to-frame table, brightness trace and missed-frame accounting cannot be supplied until fresh frames and an identifiable LED signal exist.

Detailed engineering evidence follows; this summary takes precedence over reading the receiver-only result as an end-to-end camera success.

**Inferred topology:** EVG 20 supplies the common event-101 stream observed on digital receivers 10, 30 and 40, with its local EVR 20 also logging that stream. This is strong operational evidence for multiple receivers on a common timing source, but not a verified physical port-to-device map. Other timing sources outside this inventory cannot be excluded.

Map action bit 17 (0x20000) logs events; bit 0 drives output 1 pulses. Hence 10/40 log 101 and 125, with output 1 on 125; 20/30 log and pulse output 1 on 101. Full live maps contained no other nonzero entries. Nothing in any map was changed.

**Logging capacity finding:** connected EVRs returned 85-element columns, with unused all-zero triples padded out. Their live register metadata reports `EVR:evnt:log.addr_width=8`, i.e. a 256-word register region. With the template offset of one word and three words per event, this accommodates 85 triples per register read even though the IOC arrays declare NELM=128. This indicates a tighter per-read limit than the handoff assumed; sustainable throughput and FIFO behavior still require the approved rate sweep. The disconnected prefix 50 returned default 128-element all-zero arrays, which are not hardware data.

Host receipt time minus event time ranged approximately 1.92–3.77 seconds across these logs. This includes cached initial delivery, IOC batching/transport and possible host-versus-EVG clock offset; it does not measure timing-link latency or receiver synchronization. It should be separated from hardware timestamp agreement in later analysis.
